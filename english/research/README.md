# Research behind the English app's coaching courses (Nov 2026)

Two focused reviews informed the two MVP courses. They are deliberately practical: the language coaches actually use, checked
against sources, with every uncertain point marked. The full reviews and their bibliographies:

| File | What |
| --- | --- |
| [`pitching.md`](pitching.md) | Grips, pitch design, delivery and mechanics, how pitching coaches communicate, and Spanish usage in Latin American / Dominican baseball. 55 sources. |
| [`terms-pitching.json`](terms-pitching.json) | 104 pitching terms: English, natural Spanish, alternatives, loanword status, meaning, examples, level, register, regional notes, sources. |
| [`defense.md`](defense.md) | Defensive fundamentals and their American coaching language, how US coaches instruct and check understanding, what Spanish-speaking coaches find hard, Spanish glosses. 35 sources. |
| [`terms-defense.json`](terms-defense.json) | 109 defensive and drill terms in the same format. |

The app's **Glossary** is built from the two term files (with audio in American English and Latin American Spanish), and each
entry links back to its sources.

## How the sources were read

- **Read in full** (plain text fetched in this session): the four starting sources — Driveline's *Ultimate Guide to Baseball Pitch
  Grips* and *More Than What It Seams* (seam-shifted wake and sinkers), and Baseball Savant's spin-direction and pitch-movement
  pages — plus a dozen Spanish-language articles from MLB.com en español and Diario Libre used to check terminology.
- **Seen only through search excerpts:** everything else (coaching manuals, ABCA material, motor-learning papers, other Dominican
  press). A citation to these means an excerpt supported the claim, not that the whole page was reviewed. This is marked per
  source in each bibliography.
- Statements not checked against any source are labelled **(unsourced: general coaching usage)** or **[judgement]**.

## Key findings used in the lessons

**Pitching (Learn Spanish → Pitching)**
1. *Grips are individual and judged by the movement they produce* (Driveline): small changes to finger position, pressure, thumb
   placement and depth are tested at game intensity and compared with a baseline. Lessons therefore teach the language of
   **trying and checking** ("Vamos a probar…", "¿Cómo se siente ese agarre?") rather than one correct grip.
2. *Spin and movement are measured, not guessed* (Savant): observed vs. spin-based movement, induced vertical break, spin
   direction as a clock face. Lesson 3 (planned) will teach describing a readout; Lessons 1–2 stay qualitative.
3. *Seam-shifted wake* is a real research concept (Driveline), not a coaching cue; it is mentioned only as context.
4. *Mechanics are individual and changes carry risk*: model observation ("Noté que…"), questions about feel, one small change at a
   time, agreed with the staff; never universal commands. Lesson 2 teaches observation vs. instruction explicitly.
5. *Spanish on the field mixes Spanish and English normally*: slider, sinker, cutter, bullpen, strike are used as-is in Spanish
   press; Spanish terms confirmed in full text include **la recta, la bola rápida, la recta de cuatro / dos costuras, el sinker
   (masc.), el cambio, la curva, el agarre, las costuras, la caída, el montículo, la recta cortada, la tasa de rotación, el ángulo
   del brazo, la sesión de bullpen**.
6. *Not confirmed, not taught as standard*: "la loma", "la rápida", a Spanish name for the circle change, a standard Spanish term
   for the release point. **Tú vs. usted** guidance (tú with young academy players, usted or ask with veterans and adults) is
   reasoned, not sourced, and presented as guidance.

**Defense (Learn English → Defense)**
1. American defensive instruction is **short, direct imperatives** ("Stay low.", "Set your feet.") — normal, not rude — plus
   checks for understanding ("Does that make sense?", "Show me.").
2. Motor-learning research (stated cautiously, with dissenting work cited) supports **one cue at a time**, external focus where it
   fits, and not over-coaching every rep.
3. Hard points for Spanish speakers: phrasal verbs (*get behind, work through, back up, charge*), articles and possessives with body
   parts (*your glove*), and false friends.
4. Spanish glosses for several terms (posición de listo, short hop, transfer, throwing lane, shade, cutoff/relay) were **not
   confirmed** in Spanish sources; lessons describe the idea instead of asserting one translation.
