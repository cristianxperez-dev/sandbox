# Visual reference: baseball instructional illustrations

A working reference for the illustrator rebuilding the app's instructional art: Spanish for pitching coaches, English for defensive coaches. It covers what to draw, how to draw it, and what must not be drawn as an absolute rule.

## Global style guidance (applies to all categories)

- **One idea per image.** Telestration practice says an annotation works best when it is aimed at one specific idea [S: https://www.vizrt.com/blog/telestration-coaching-viz-libero-teams/]. In practice: one arrow, one label, one highlighted body part.
- **Show sequences as steps, not one cluttered image.** Cognitive-load research recommends presenting dynamic information serially rather than all at once [S: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7794790]. A storyboard rule of thumb: add a panel when the action is unclear, and remove one that repeats information without showing movement or timing [S: https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/5-storyboard-checks-before-you-animate].
- **Viewpoint.** One technical-illustration thesis argues that a three-quarter view from slightly below shows physical actions best [S: https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/nozawa-thesis/20055214]. Coaching-observation guidance warns that a front view is poor for throwing skills [S: https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/how-to-observe-a-skill]. **[I]** For throwing and pitching, default to a side view from the throwing-arm side or the glove side, and use a front view only for alignment checks.
- **Handedness.** **[I]** Draw a right-handed thrower by default and keep that consistent across the app. Mirrored left-handed versions should be deliberate, labelled assets, never an accidental flip. A flipped right-hander also flips the glove hand, the seam text and the stitching direction.
- **Treatment [I].** Clean line art with flat or two-tone fills. Realistic proportions, adult professional athlete, plain uniform, no team logos or real-player likeness. A neutral background, or a minimal field cue such as a dirt line or a base. Muted annotation in one accent colour. The style should read as editorial and technical, not cartoon.

## A. Pitch grips

**References**
1. Driveline, "How to Throw a Sinker or Two-Seam Fastball" (2020): both fingers on the seams gives the two-seam orientation, and for a sinker "you may move the index finger slightly inwards". It aims for some sidespin, roughly a 1:30–2:00 axis, with just enough backspin [S: https://www.drivelinebaseball.com/2020/06/how-to-throw-a-sinker-or-two-seam-fastball/].
2. Wikipedia, "Four-seam fastball": index and middle fingers "on or across" the outward-facing horseshoe seam [S: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-seam_fastball]. Baseball-Reference Bullpen: "fingers across the seams" [S: https://www.baseball-reference.com/bullpen/Four-seam_fastball].
3. Art of Manliness grip guide: the thumb tip rests on smooth leather, not on a seam [S: https://www.artofmanliness.com/skills/manly-know-how/7-baseball-pitching-grips-every-man-should-know/]. Active.com: fingertip pads about ½ in apart [S: https://www.active.com/baseball/articles/how-to-teach-the-four-seam-fastball-grip].
4. Circle change, cutter and slider descriptions [S: https://rocklandpeakperformance.com/?p=27577; https://www.topvelocity.net/2014/09/08/top-10-professional-pitching-grips/; https://brucebolt.us/blogs/news/baseball-pitch-grips].
5. Not read (blocked): https://drivelinebaseball.com/blogs/blog/ultimate-guide-baseball-pitch-grips and https://help.drivelinebaseball.com/portal/en/kb/articles/pitch-grips-fastballs-27-1-2022. Check them for camera angle and for their current fastball wording.

**Grip content (conservative)**
- **Four-seam:** index and middle fingers across the horseshoe, the part of the seam that bulges furthest apart. Fingertip pads sit on or just over the seam, roughly a finger's width apart; sources give anywhere from about ½ in up to a finger's width [S, refs 2–3]. Thumb under the ball, on smooth leather [S]. One guide describes the bent thumb and the folded ring and little fingers forming a supporting "triangle" underneath [S: https://baseball.epicsports.com/baseball-pitching-styles.html].
- **Two-seam / sinker:** fingers run along the seams where they come closest together, with the thumb beneath [S: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-seam_fastball]. Driveline: both fingers on the seams, with the index finger optionally slightly inside for a sinker [S, ref 1].
- **Circle change:** thumb and index finger form an "OK" circle on the side of the ball, and the other three fingers hold the ball. The ball usually sits deeper in the hand [S, ref 4].
- **Cutter:** a fastball grip shifted slightly off-centre toward the glove side, with more pressure on the outer part of the ball. Some sources set the fingers closer together [S, ref 4].
- **Slider:** off-centre, with pressure on the outer third of the ball. Sources differ on the details; one describes a middle-finger-and-thumb squeeze with the thumb under the ball [S, ref 4].

**What makes grip references effective [I]:** a real ball at true scale, sharp red stitching, and the hand cropped at the wrist on a plain background. The commonest convention is two views of the same grip: one from the **back/side of the hand**, the hitter's view of the top fingers, and one from **below** to show the thumb.

**Anatomical details:** fingertip pads (not the middle finger segments) touching the leather. A visible gap between ball and palm on fastballs is common in grip photos, but no source I could read confirms it, so treat it as **[I]**. The ring and little fingers fold against the side of the ball. Knuckles stay relaxed, not clenched white.

**Recommended views:** (1) top or back of hand, fingers pointing up as at release; (2) underside to show the thumb; (3) optionally a side view from the thumb side for the circle change.

**Common mistakes:** fingers drawn too long relative to the ball (a baseball is 9–9.25 in around, roughly 2.9 in across), so check the ratio. Seams drawn as a simple equator. The thumb drawn on top of the ball. The circle-change "OK" drawn on the front of the ball instead of its side. The same seam layout used for four-seam and two-seam art.

**Coaching cautions:** grips are personal. Driveline frames grip choice by spin axis and seam orientation rather than one correct hold [S, ref 1]. Even the cause of two-seam movement is debated; American Scientist reports wind-tunnel tests showing no significant lift difference between two- and four-seam orientations [S: https://www.americanscientist.org/template/IssueTOC/issue/348], while seam-shifted-wake work argues otherwise [S: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seam-shifted_wake]. Caption grips as "a common grip", never "the grip". **Never draw arrows that claim movement direction or spin axis** unless a coach approves them.

## B. The hand (parts and finger names)

**References:** Lecturio, hand anatomy [S: https://www.lecturio.com/concepts/hand/]; WSU gross anatomy textbook, ch. 11 [S: https://learning.medicine.wsu.edu/gross-anatomy/textbook/11-hand]; handsurgeonsnyc.com [S: https://handsurgeonsnyc.com/hand-anatomy/].

- **Labels to use:** thumb; index finger (forefinger); middle finger (long finger); ring finger; little finger (clinical texts prefer this to "pinky") [S]. Joints: MCP (base knuckle), PIP (middle knuckle), DIP (end knuckle); the thumb has only one IP joint and two phalanges [S]. Palm features: the thenar eminence (the pad at the base of the thumb) and the hypothenar eminence (the little-finger side) [S]. "Fingertip pad" is the everyday term; I could not confirm a clinical name [S note].
- **Spanish/English pairs** (pulgar, índice, medio/corazón, anular, meñique; yema, nudillo, palma) are standard vocabulary, not sourced here. Have a native-speaking coach confirm regional usage. **[I]**
- **View:** palm side and back side, open and relaxed, fingers slightly spread. Thin leader lines to labels outside the silhouette. No labels on the skin.
- **Avoid:** medical-textbook realism such as tendons or bones; too many labels on one image (keep it to 5–8); left/right confusion. Label which hand it is, normally the throwing hand.

## C. The baseball and its seams

**References:** Library of Congress blog: 108 double stitches, 9–9.25 in around, 5–5.25 oz [S: https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/2018/03/baseballs-the-heart-of-the-matter/]. Wikipedia: two saddle-shaped (often called "figure-eight") pieces of leather, red-dyed thread; cowhide replaced horsehide in 1974 [S: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_(ball)]. The phrase "figure-eight" did not appear in what I read [S note].

- **Geometry:** one continuous seam spirals around the ball. From most angles you see a **horseshoe (U)**, where the seam bows widest, and a **narrow "neck"** where the two seam lines run closest and nearly parallel [S/I]. "Four-seam" means four seam lines cross the plane of rotation per revolution with the fingers across the horseshoe. "Two-seam" means two cross, with the fingers along the narrow seams [S: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-seam_fastball].
- **Effective depiction [I]:** a 3D-correct seam built from an actual ball or a 3D model, never freehand; the "tennis-ball curve" is the classic error. Stitches drawn as a chevron (V) pattern running consistently in one direction along the seam, with a slightly raised seam. Use a consistent count, but do not literally draw 108 at small sizes.
- **Views:** a horseshoe-facing view for the four-seam lesson, and the narrow seams running vertically for the two-seam lesson. A pair of orientation diagrams with a curved spin arrow is acceptable. Keep any axis arrow generic and do not imply a movement outcome.
- **Avoid:** stitches that change direction partway along the seam; inconsistent seam paths between grip images; extra seam loops.

## D. Pitching delivery positions and "front side opening early"

**References**
1. Biomechanics phases (ASMI/Fleisig convention, as summarised in reviews): **wind-up** (first motion → maximum knee height); **stride** (max knee height → front foot contact); **arm cocking** (foot contact → maximum shoulder external rotation, MER); **arm acceleration** (MER → ball release); **arm deceleration** (release → end of internal rotation); **follow-through** (→ balanced fielding position) [S: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6542879; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5435152]. Some papers split cocking into early and late, and phase boundaries vary slightly between papers [S].
2. Arm slot/angle: Statcast reports arm angle with 0° = sidearm, 90° = straight over the top, and negative values = submarine, averaged over a pitcher's fastballs [S: https://www.mlb.com/amp/news/how-arm-slot-and-arm-angle-affect-pitches.html; https://mlb.com/news/the-range-of-pitcher-arm-angles-in-the-2024-mlb-postseason]. Arm angle is not release height; a tall three-quarter pitcher can release higher than a short over-the-top one [S].
3. "Flying open": the front shoulder or hip rotating too early, before foot plant [S: https://baseballtips.com/?p=220764; https://intercom.help/upliftlabs/en/articles/12481739-flying-open-pitching]. The counterpoint, that "stay closed" can backfire [S: https://floridabaseballarmory.com/the-stay-closed-cue-could-be-wrecking-your-delivery/; https://armorypitching.com/?p=1993].
4. FanGraphs, visual scouting primer (pitching) [S: https://blogs.fangraphs.com/a-visual-scouting-primer-pitching-part-one]: a model for how analysts show deliveries frame by frame.

**Recommended panels:** (1) leg lift / balance at max knee height; (2) stride, hands separating; (3) **foot strike**, the most useful teaching frame; (4) arm cocking / MER; (5) release; (6) finish / follow-through. For a phone screen, 4 panels (lift → foot strike → release → finish) is usually enough **[I]**.

**Anatomy and baseball details:** at foot strike, the stride foot is planted, the throwing arm is up and the trunk is still somewhat closed. At MER the forearm is laid back behind the head and the trunk is turning. At release the front knee is braced or flexed and the trunk tilts forward and/or to the glove side. The glove arm's position varies (tucked, pulled or stabilised) [I]. The pitching rubber, mound slope and stride direction toward the plate should be visible in side views.

**Views:** a side view from first base for a right-hander (the open side, showing the chest) for phases; a view from behind home or from second base for stride direction and "opening early".

**"Front side opening early" (comparison pair):** two panels at the *same* moment, foot strike: (a) chest and front hip still mostly facing third base (RHP); (b) chest already facing home with the arm lagging behind. Use an overhead inset or a view from behind the plate, with a single arc arrow on the shoulders. **[I]**

**Avoid:** a stiff straight-arm "windmill"; a stride foot landing across the body unless that is the point; a cartoonishly high leg kick; drawing MER with an anatomically impossible lay-back; implying that the arm-cocking drawing is the "injury moment" without context.

**Coaching cautions:** phase names differ (biomechanics "arm cocking" vs coaching "loading"/"high cock"). Lead-leg block is not universal [S: https://velou.substack.com/p/lead-knee-extension-isnt-universal]. "Flying open" may be a symptom of a closed start or stride direction, not a cause [S, ref 3]. Captions should describe positions ("chest facing home at foot strike"), not prescribe one cure.

## E. Infield ready position (pre-pitch, creep)

**References:** MLB.com, "'Engage': Infielders work on pre-pitch setup" (Phillies, Juan Castro): syncing the setup with the pitcher, with the last ~3 ft before the plate as the critical window [S: https://www.mlb.com/news/phillies-infielders-work-on-setup-drills]. Perry Hill's Infield System manual [S: https://goldglovedefense.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Perry-Hills-Infield-System-Training-Manual.pdf]. TeamSnap, infielder setup [S: https://teamsnap.com/community/skills-drills/baseball/fielding/637-infielder-setup-and-ready-positions]. Chad Moeller, pre-pitch setup [S: https://chadmoellerbaseball.com/pre-pitch-set-up-for-an-infielder/].

- **Content:** feet about shoulder-width or wider, knees soft, weight on the balls of the feet, a short creep or walk-in step timed to the pitch, set as the ball enters the hitting zone [S]. One source quotes a "55–60 %" weight split; that figure appears on one site only, so do not illustrate a number [S note]. One infield outline describes middle infielders with the glove "presented to the hitter" [S: https://tshq.bluesombrero.com/portals/29505/docs/skill%20dev/field/infield_play_outline.pdf].
- **Show as:** a 2-panel sequence (creep step → set/engaged) from a front-quarter view, with the hitter's direction indicated.
- **Avoid:** hands on knees at the moment of the pitch (that is a between-pitch rest); straight legs; heels flat with the weight back; a false step toward the hitter [S: https://www.dugoutcaptain.com/?p=242].
- **Caution:** creep styles vary (walk-in, hop or split-step timing, open or square feet). Show one example labelled "one common setup".

## F. Fielding a ground ball

**References:** Perry Hill's "6 Fs" (Feet, Field, Funnel, Footwork, Fire, Follow) [S: https://thefarmleague.com/baseball/drills/page-1; https://goldglovedefense.com/base-content/6fs-5-fire]. Little League, infield drill progression [S: https://www.littleleague.org/university/articles/infield-drill-progression/]. Human Kinetics, fundamentals of fielding a ground ball [S: https://canada.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/fundamentals-of-fielding-a-ground-ball]. Cal Ripken ground-ball drills (back "parallel to ground") [S: https://tshq.bluesombrero.com/portals/24519/docs/cal%20ripken/cal%20ripken_youth%20coaching_drills%20ground%20ball10.pdf].

- **Content:** a wide base, butt down, hands out front. The "triangle" is the two feet as its base with the glove at the apex, out in front [S]. The ball is caught out front so the eyes see ball and glove together [S]. Then the ball is funnelled to the centre of the body (to the belly or throwing side) as the throwing hand covers it [S].
- **Anatomy:** hip hinge plus knee flexion with the spine long. The "flat back" cue is common [S], but the drawing should show bend at the hips *and* knees, not a rounded spine. Head down over the glove, eyes on the ball, glove-side foot often slightly ahead (varies, **[I]**).
- **Views:** a three-quarter front view from the hitter's side, plus an overhead "triangle" inset with the feet as two marks and the glove as the apex.
- **Avoid:** the glove under the body between the feet; the throwing hand clamped on top of the glove before the catch (many coaches show the throwing hand near or beside the glove, open); legs together; arms locked straight. One source warns against reaching so far out front that the arm locks [S].
- **Caution:** the order of "funnel" and "footwork" and the exact foot stagger differ between sources [S]. Pro instruction increasingly teaches fielding on the move, with a one-hand forehand or backhand when appropriate. Do not caption "always two hands".

## G. Glove position by ball height (fingers up / fingers down)

**What sources actually say [S]:** youth materials teach "above the waist, fingers up; below the waist, fingers down" [S: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1WM_Zkw9OmB9B3fSjqRFxWkKht8gBEeX1/export?format=pdf; https://tshq.bluesombrero.com/portals/29505/docs/skill%20dev/field/infield_mechanics_and_fundamentals.pdf]. **Disagreement on low balls:** one source says fingers down with the fingertips touching the ground; another says the *back of the glove's fingers* rests in the dirt at a slight angle, because fingers pointing straight down "is impossible to do and still reach out" [S: same search set; https://community.hsbaseballweb.com/topic/staying-down-on-the-ball]. One outline describes the glove at roughly "6–7 o'clock" with the fingers spread [S: https://tshq.bluesombrero.com/portals/29505/docs/skill%20dev/field/infield_play_outline.pdf]. Short hops are attacked with the glove out front, "throwing the palm at the ball" [S: https://rawlings.com/four-training-tips-infielders.html].

**Accurate phrasing to use:**
- "Ball above the belt: usually fingers up. Ball below the belt: usually fingers down, with the glove open and its face toward the ball."
- "On a ground ball, the glove starts low and opens toward the ball. It is easier to come up than to go down." **[I]**, a common cue; verify with a coach.
- **Belt-high and in-between balls:** sources found give no single rule. **[I]** Players commonly turn the glove sideways (thumb up or down) or backhand it. Mark this as a judgement zone, not a rule.
- **Backhand:** the glove face turns toward the ball with the fingers angled down and forward and the thumb side up. I found no source specifying an angle [S note].

**Illustration:** a single figure with three glove positions (high, belt, low), using a dashed waist line as a reference band, or three small panels. Ghost the in-between position in a lighter tone with a "depends" note. A **front view** shows the glove face best.

**Avoid:** literally vertical fingers jammed into the ground; the glove face pointing at the sky on a low ball; a hard waist "line" implying a binary switch.

## H. Throwing setup for an infielder

**References:** "exit footwork" R-L-shuffle-throw (or pop-shuffle-throw); for a right-hander, the right foot steps first "ankle eye toward the target", then the left [S: https://dugoutcaptain.com/drill/fundamentals-ground-balls/]. The crow hop is presented mainly as an outfield move, with the back foot "up and over" [S: https://dugoutcaptain.com/?p=312]. Hill's "Footwork: replacing the feet creates distance and direction to the target" and "Follow" [S, Perry Hill refs]. Shoulders square to the target, feet under the body [S: https://dugoutcaptain.com/?p=312].

**Recommended sequence (RH thrower; 4 panels):** (1) field: catch out front; (2) transfer: the ball comes to the body's centre and the hands separate; (3) replace or shuffle the feet: right foot steps toward or behind the left, ankle facing the target, so the **glove-side shoulder and front hip point at the target**; (4) stride and throw, then follow toward the target.

**Views:** a side view perpendicular to the throwing line, plus an overhead footprint diagram: numbered footprints (1 R, 2 L, …) with a target arrow to first base.

**Details:** the throw line from the shortstop or third base to first base is diagonal across the diamond, so footprints must align with *that* line, not with home plate. The transfer can happen in the glove at the centre of the body; the ball should be visible at the moment of transfer.

**Avoid:** both feet parallel to the baseline; a full outfield crow hop drawn for a routine infield play; throwing with the chest facing the target before the stride.

**Cautions:** variations are real and situational: shuffle vs replace vs crow hop; throwing off the back foot or on the run on slow rollers; flips and underhand tosses; second-base pivots. Label this "a routine-play sequence".

## I. Footwork: first step and angles (forehand and backhand)

**References:** practice notes: the drop step is common for gaining ground, crossovers for balls outside normal range [S: https://sites.google.com/view/bombersbaseball/class-room/practice-notes/fall-2025/majors-practice-notes/8202025]. Human Kinetics describes the crossover as the main side-to-side move [S: https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/fielding-ground-balls]. Coaches Insider: first-step reads and a forehand drill to remove false steps; Billy Boyer forehand/backhand drill [S: https://coachesinsider.com/baseball/infielders-first-step-read-with-kj-hendricks-milwaukee-brewers; https://coachesinsider.com/baseball/infielders-forehand-backhand-groundball-drill-with-billy-boyer-detroit-tigers]. Backhand: stay low, glove knee at or below the glove [S: same search set]. Read the hop: attack the short hop, or back up for the long hop [S].

- **Best shown as overhead diagrams:** fielder start dot → ball path arrow → curved route ("banana" or angle route) to the intercept point, with footprints for the first step (pivot plus crossover). Pair each diagram with one side-view pose of the backhand or forehand.
- **Backhand pose [I]:** for a RH infielder going to his right, the glove-side (left) foot is often across, the glove reaches back and open, and the right foot plants to throw. Variations: the left-foot-forward "true" backhand vs a right-foot-plant backhand. Coaches differ, so mark it as a variation.
- **Avoid:** straight-line arrows that imply running directly sideways; a first step backward or toward the plate shown as the default.
- **Caution:** drop step vs crossover as the "first step" is genuinely contested [S].

## J. Infield positioning and shading (diagrams)

**References:** the 2023 MLB rule: four infielders, two on each side of second base, with their feet on the infield dirt (not the outfield grass) when the pitcher begins his delivery; outfielders are unrestricted [S: https://www.cbssports.com/mlb/news/mlb-rule-changes-what-to-know-about-the-extreme-shift-ban-what-defensive-tricks-are-still-allowed; https://www.foxsports.com/articles/mlb/mlb-curtails-infield-shift-hopes-for-more-singles-speed]. The exact foot wording differs between reports ("both feet within the outer boundary" vs "at least one foot"), so check the Official Baseball Rules text [S note].

**Conventions [I]:** a top-down diamond with home plate at the bottom; the dirt arc and grass drawn in two neutral tones; fielders as labelled circles (position numbers 1–9 or abbreviations: 1B, 2B, SS, 3B); a "standard" ghost position plus an arrow to the shaded spot; a batter-side icon (L/R) in the corner; a dashed line marking second base for the 2023 rule. Use a consistent scale, base paths 90 ft.

**Avoid:** infielders on the grass in examples that claim to be legal for MLB; three infielders on one side; mixing an outfield shift into an infield lesson without saying so.

**Caution:** amateur and international leagues may not use the MLB restriction. Caption which ruleset applies.

## K. Hitting (future: brief)

**References:** "door-knocking" knuckles aligned, or a box grip; firm without tension; finger-dominant rather than deep in the palm [S: https://grandavehousing.calpoly.edu/blog/baseball-batting-stance-and-swing-a-comprehensive-guide-1764808766; https://www.gripboost.com/blogs/news/baseball-batting-tips-to-improve-your-hitting-game]. Phases: coaching (load, stride, swing, follow-through) vs research (preparatory, stance, stride, drive, bat acceleration, follow-through, adapted from Fleisig et al.) [S: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4675191]. Contact out in front [S].

- **Show:** a grip close-up (two hands, the knuckle lines highlighted); stance from the pitcher's view and from the side; a 4-panel swing (stance → load/stride → foot down/launch → contact → finish, trimmed to 4).
- **Caution:** grip alignment, stance width, hand height and stride (leg kick vs toe tap vs no stride) all vary widely. No "correct" stance.

## Production implications

| Format | Best for |
|---|---|
| **(a) Single pose** | Infield ready position (set); fielding position "triangle" at the catch; backhand and forehand catch poses; hitting stance; pitcher at foot strike (the single most informative delivery frame) |
| **(b) Sequence, 2–4 panels** | Creep → set; field → transfer → feet → throw; pitching delivery (lift → foot strike → release → finish); "opening early" vs "on time" comparison pair at foot strike; swing phases; glove height (high / belt / low) |
| **(c) Overhead diagram** | Infield positioning and shading (2023 rule); angles and routes to the ball; throwing footprints aligned to first base; the fielding "triangle" inset; stride direction toward the plate |
| **(d) Close-up hand / ball / glove** | All pitch grips (two views each); hand parts; baseball seams (four-seam vs two-seam orientation); glove anatomy (see below); bat grip knuckles; glove face orientation (fingers up, down or sideways) |

**Glove anatomy close-up (for category G/F lessons).** Parts with sourced definitions: heel (lower palm side; protection, sets the break), web (between thumb and index finger; styles such as H-web, I-web, single post, trapeze, basket), back, palm lining, welting (thin leather along the fingers), lacing (connects the fingers and holds the shape) [S: https://www.dickssportinggoods.com/s/baseball-softball-glove-buying-guide; https://keymancollectibles.com/baseballgovewebglossary.htm; https://www.justballgloves.com/product/rawlings-heart-of-the-hide-color-sync-2-0-11-75--baseball-glove--pro315-7slc/28966/]. Pocket: infield gloves have a shallower pocket for quick transfers [S: https://www.baseballmonkey.com/homerun-glove-sizing-chart]. Size: infield gloves are commonly about 11–12 in. Retailers quote 11–11.5 in for 2B/SS and up to 11.75 in for 3B, and Rawlings markets 11.75 in for shortstops and third basemen [S: same; https://www.scheels.com/e/post/baseball-glove-guide.html]. I found no sourced definitions of "binding" or the "finger stalls" [S note]; use them only with a coach-checked caption. Draw an infield glove with an open web (H-web or I-web) and a shallow pocket, front (palm) view plus back view, with label leaders.

**General checklist for every illustration [I]:** correct handedness; ball at true scale against the hand or glove; a seam path checked against a real ball; one annotation idea; caption says "common" or "one way" where the coaching varies; a qualified coach signs off before release.
